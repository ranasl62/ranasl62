About

Making diagnostic AI more reliable, explainable, and reusable

Md Rana Hossain · Detroit, Michigan · M.S., Computer Science, Maharishi International University · Senior Full Stack Engineer, Volkswagen Group of America

My research develops explainable deep-learning methods for early diagnosis from medical images and clinical data, and the reliability tools that make those models safer to evaluate: calibrated probabilities, leakage-aware validation, and risk-controlled deferral when a model is uncertain.

My first-author work covers multimodal imaging diagnosis for osteoporosis. I have co-authored Springer Nature and IEEE publications on heart disease, cancer, diabetic eye disease, Alzheimer’s disease, and gastrointestinal and brain imaging, and I serve as a peer reviewer for biomedical and AI-in-health venues.

I maintain the open-source EHR Risk Framework and wrote a methods book on early disease detection and clinical risk prediction. In industry, I deliver production LangChain/LangGraph retrieval and agent systems at Volkswagen Group of America.

Healthcare methods are research and education only—not a medical device and not bedside deployment.

Where they meet

The research asks whether a model can be evaluated without leakage, with calibrated probabilities, and with an explanation of each prediction. The EHR Risk Framework is the open software for that evaluation loop on longitudinal risk modeling. It is built with ordinary production practice—FastAPI, Angular, a pytest suite, and GitHub Actions. It is not a medical device.

Research focus

Trustworthy diagnostic AI: explainable and calibrated

I am advancing AI methods that support earlier, more reliable diagnosis of high-burden diseases, and releasing the validation tools so that other U.S. laboratories, students, and public-health research partners can reuse them.

1. Diagnostic models

Explainable deep-learning and multimodal models for early diagnosis from medical images and clinical data, including osteoporosis, colorectal cancer, diabetic retinopathy, Alzheimer’s disease, gastrointestinal disease, brain tumor, and heart disease.

Examples: first-author knee osteoporosis framework; CRuSE-Heart (Springer).

2. A reliability layer

Calibrated probabilities, leakage-aware evaluation, and risk-controlled abstention, so a model can report how much to trust each prediction and defer when it should.

Examples: CRuSE-Heart risk-controlled selective ensembles; calibration and leakage audits in the EHR Risk Framework.

3. Open validation infrastructure

Free, citable, reproducible tools for external validation, calibration reporting, and explainability analysis, for university labs, nonprofit research groups, and students.

Example: EHR Risk Framework (MIT license, public documentation, live demo, Zenodo DOI).

Why this matters

  • Heart disease and cancer are the two leading causes of death in the United States, and Alzheimer disease is also among the ten leading causes (CDC NCHS FastStats, final 2024 data; NCHS Data Brief 548).
  • Published diagnostic-AI results are often inflated by data leakage, small single-source datasets, and uncalibrated probabilities. Clinicians and researchers need to know when a model can be trusted, not only how accurate it looked in one study.
  • Calibration and repeatable evaluation are how this work approaches two characteristics named in the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0): valid and reliable, and explainable and interpretable. That is a research-method alignment only, not product certification.
  • Open methods and tools raise research capacity across many institutions, so the benefit does not stay inside one organization.

Roadmap

Planned work in the United States

Concrete next steps that build directly on completed work.

  1. Journal-level, externally validated diagnosis. Extend the first-author knee osteoporosis framework into a journal article with testing on additional public datasets.
  2. Reliability layer for imaging. Extend calibration and risk-controlled abstention from tabular and EHR settings to imaging classifiers, and report the results with external validation.
  3. First-author methods papers in preparation. A controlled audit of temporal leakage and inflated discrimination in EHR risk prediction, and calibrated probabilities with risk-controlled abstention.
  4. Open release and community use. Add imaging-focused modules, tutorials, and reproducibility benchmarks to the open framework, and seek collaborations with U.S. clinicians and research groups.

Collaboration inquiries from U.S. universities, clinicians, and research labs are welcome. Get in touch.

Open software

Research tools released for reuse

EHR Risk Framework

Creator and maintainer. Open workbench for longitudinal risk modeling: index/horizon cohorts, patient-level splits, leakage audits, isotonic calibration (Brier, ECE), TreeSHAP, fairness slices, reproducible JSON manifests, FastAPI + Angular UI. Being extended toward imaging-diagnosis reliability analysis.

The citable archive is Zenodo version 1.0.0 (22 July 2026), which is also the version in CITATION.cff. The concept DOI always points at the latest version. The version DOI identifies that 1.0.0 record. The repository includes a pytest suite and a GitHub Actions workflow. GitHub showed 24 stars and 6 forks on 26 October 2026.
